02 / Big question

How can drone technology be used to solve real-world challenges in schools by conserving water resources?

03 / Our focus

Water conservation

Leaks can go unnoticed on large campuses.

A school has many tanks and pipes. Some sit on roofs or behind gardens, where nobody walks past every day, so a slow drip can keep going until someone happens to see it.

Our project shows how a cheap camera drone could help people check these places, and how an alert could reach the team when a drop is detected.

04 / Inspection plan

Four places to check

Card 1 of 4

Water tanks

What we check
The sides and base of each tank, and the pipe joints around it.
What the drone looks for
Wet patches, drips, dark stains on the ground, and cracks or loose fittings.
How often
Example: once a week (team to confirm)

Card 2 of 4

Irrigation

What we check
The irrigation line from Tank 3 and its small sprinkler risers.
What the drone looks for
Puddles along the pipe, a sprinkler that will not stop, and grass much greener than the grass around it.
How often
Example: once a week (team to confirm)

Card 3 of 4

Garden areas

What we check
The soil and raised beds in the garden, next to Tank 2.
What the drone looks for
Soil that stays wet when it should be dry, and beds that look drier than the others.
How often
Example: twice a week (team to confirm)

Card 4 of 4

Rooftop tanks

What we check
Tank 1 on the roof, its lid and its overflow pipe. People rarely see these.
What the drone looks for
Water on the roof, marks left by overflowing water, and a lid out of place.
How often
Example: once a month (team to confirm)

05 / Inside the drone

From the drone to the app

Card 1 of 4

The drone

Shown for demonstration

A generic camera quadcopter with four rotors, the kind of cheap drone a school could buy. We do not assume it has a programming interface, so nothing on this website controls a real drone. It is a demo only.

Card 2 of 4

The ESP board

ESP board (demo)

A small green circuit board under the drone in our model. It is a labelled demo part that shows where a small computer could sit. In the code, a function called readTankSensor() marks the place where a real sensor reading would go.

Card 3 of 4

The camera

A small camera under the front of the drone. It lets an adult pilot look closely at tanks, pipes and soil from above. Faces are blurred before any photos are shared.

Card 4 of 4

How the alert travels

  1. Water sensor at the tank: notices a drop
  2. ESP board: reads the sensor
  3. Wi-Fi: carries the message
  4. Laptop receiver: stores the alert
  5. App on phone or laptop: shows the alert

In this demo, the website's simulator stands in for the sensor and the ESP board.

08 / Safety and ethics

People first, drone second

The drone on this website is a 3D model. If a real one is ever flown at school, these rules come first.

Flight rules

We follow local drone rules and the school's permission. If the rules or the weather say no, we do not fly.

Adult pilot and spotter

An adult pilot controls the drone, and a spotter keeps watching the drone and the area around it.

Approval first

No flight happens without approval from the school. Approval: [Approval reference]

Privacy

Faces are blurred before photos are shared. We only use pictures that help us check tanks, pipes and gardens.

10 / Our answer

A drone can help a school look after its water

This project is a demo, so these are our aims, not measured results.

Safer

A drone can look at rooftop tanks and high pipes from the air, so people do not need to climb up just to check them.

Smarter

Regular inspections can show the team where to look first, so their time goes to the tanks, pipes and garden beds that need it.

More responsive

An alert can reach the app on a phone or laptop when a sensor reports a drop. This aims to help someone act on a leak sooner.
